Report content: notice and action

Version 1.0 (beta) · Last updated 11 August 2026

How to report

Send reports to luke.a.purnell@gmail.com. We accept reports in English. You can use this route to report anything you believe is unlawful, including copyright infringement.

One thing to know before you write. Subbox is a private storage and playback service: a user's library is visible only to that user, and there is no feature that publishes a library or a track to the public. So there is usually no public URL to point us at. Tell us whatever you do have — an account name, an artist and title, how you came to know the material is there — and we will investigate from our side.

What a report should contain

Reports that contain the following are the fastest for us to act on, and this is what the law expects of a notice in the UK and the EU:

  1. Why the content is unlawful — a clear explanation, including which right you say is infringed or which law is broken.
  2. Where it is — the exact location, as precisely as you can manage: a URL if there is one, otherwise the account name, track title and artist.
  3. Who you are — your name and email address, and if you are acting for a rightsholder, who you act for and in what capacity. (You do not have to give your name if the report concerns certain criminal offences against children.)
  4. A statement of good faith — confirming that the information in your report is accurate and complete to the best of your knowledge.

Please make reports carefully. A knowingly false or bad-faith report may make you liable for damages.

What we do with it

  1. We acknowledge it. We will confirm receipt of your report by email without undue delay.
  2. We assess it in a timely, diligent and non-arbitrary way. We do not use automated decision-making for this.
  3. We act. If the content is unlawful we will act expeditiously to remove it or disable access to it, and we may suspend or terminate the account responsible.
  4. We explain. We will tell you what we decided and why. If we removed or restricted content, we will also give the affected user a statement of the reasons, and tell them how to challenge it.

We keep a record of every report we receive and what we did about it, including the dates.

If your content was removed

If we removed or disabled access to your content and you think we were wrong, reply to the notice we sent you, or write to luke.a.purnell@gmail.com, explaining why. A person will review it and we will restore the content if the objection is well-founded. This does not affect your right to go to court, or to use any out-of-court dispute settlement body available to you.

Repeat infringers

We terminate, in appropriate circumstances, the accounts of users who repeatedly infringe copyright.

Copyright reports from the United States (DMCA)

If you are sending a notice under the US Digital Millennium Copyright Act, please include all of the following, as section 512(c)(3) requires:

  1. a physical or electronic signature of a person authorised to act for the owner of the right;
  2. identification of the copyrighted work you say has been infringed;
  3. identification of the material you say is infringing, with enough information for us to locate it;
  4. your name, address, telephone number and email address;
  5. a statement that you have a good faith belief that the use is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law;
  6. a statement, made under penalty of perjury, that the information in the notice is accurate and that you are authorised to act for the owner of the right.

Send DMCA notices to luke.a.purnell@gmail.com, the same address as any other report.

If you receive a DMCA notice about your content and believe it was mistaken, you may send us a counter-notice containing the elements set out in section 512(g)(3). We will forward it to the person who complained and, unless they tell us they have filed a court action, we may restore the content after the statutory period.

Other points of contact

Everything else: luke.a.purnell@gmail.com.
